The customer invoiced after its establishment closed found by place in the legacy export

Goal Offer each ERP customer the SIRET of its establishment, and spot the ones INSEE says are closed, by crossing the customer export with INSEE's public files through the GPS point.

With e-invoicing, the customer's SIREN becomes a mandatory mention. The legacy export has no reliable SIRET; INSEE publishes every establishment with its state and its GPS point. No common key: the link is the place.

  • 5 customers get an active SIRET within 30 m
  • 3 closed establishments within 30 m of a customer, each for a person to read
  • one customer is still invoiced after the closure, with no active establishment beside it

The customer export and two INSEE files, each as delivered

Three CSV files, one connector each.

  • customers.csv: the ERP, with a decimal comma and the date of the last invoice
  • sirene-geolocalisation.csv: INSEE's format. It gives y_latitude and x_longitude in degrees, beside Lambert 93
  • sirene-etablissements.csv: the Sirene stock, 54 comma-separated columns
  • parse-coordinates: reads each pair as numbers; within: the rectangle of metropolitan France

Limits

  • The wording matters, and the model's (qwen2.5-coder:7b) path varies with the card: across the preparation rehearsals, "… d'un établissement fermé?" got an abstention with no SQL, a query the guard refused, or the right list. The guard refuses a measure made through a sub-query, even a right one. All questions were asked in French.
  • INSEE's point is not always the door. With qualite_xy 12 the street is sure but the position along it is random; with 22 the street itself is only probable: within 120.4 m for C-106's print shop.
  • The link is the place, not the name: "Boulang. des Pentes" and "BOULANGERIE DES PENTES" are compared by eye. In a shopping centre, 30 m covers dozens of establishments: the list becomes a list of candidates.
  • The answer's sentence is written by the model: here it says "of each customer" when two customers have none, mixes a customer's name with the SIRET next door, misspells a sign. The table and the SQL are what counts.
  • The national files, 1.1 GB and 2.9 GB compressed, were not loaded: their volume is not measured here.
  • INSEE announces the end of the geolocation CSV in the second half of 2027, for Parquet only, which csv-file does not read.
  • The November 2026 file codes the state A or F. The Sirene V4 API splits it into several codes.
  • Invented establishments and SIRETs: they fail the Luhn check on purpose, none is real. The files keep INSEE's real format.

The customers within 20 km of the depot measured from the legacy export

Goal Ask which customers are within 20 km of a depot and get the list measured from the ERP's coordinates, with its SQL, or a doubt when the centre cannot be found, never an invented figure.

The sales team wants to know which customers a depot can deliver within the day. The legacy ERP exports one GPS point per customer, with a decimal comma.

  • the hub measures the distance; the model does not write it
  • the SQL comes with the answer
  • a city with no depot and no customer gets no invented coordinates

The customers export and the depots, coordinates read at the door

Two CSV files, one connector each. The coordinates arrive with a decimal comma.

  • parse-coordinates: reads latitude and longitude together, as numbers
  • within: south, west, north, east. The rectangle of metropolitan France
  • reason: the column that says why a point was emptied
  1. 1
    17 = 17

    Every customer is written, with or without a point.

  2. 2
    C-017

    0;0, left by a geocoder: outside the rectangle, emptied and counted.

What this step proves

Coordinates become numbers at the door; an unusable point is emptied with its reason.

Within 20 km of the Lyon depot, then of a typed pair

The answer shows its SQL: the depot is found by its commune, Lyon, and connector.distance_m measures from its point.

  • from the Lyon depot: 8 customers
  • from 45.764, 4.8357, the centre of Lyon: 7 customers, Chasse-sur-Rhône drops out
  1. 1
    Chasse-sur-Rhône

    Within 20 km of the depot, not of the centre of Lyon.

  2. 2
    Computed apart

    data/distances.py, run by the replay, gives the same lists.

No depot and no customer is in Clermont-Ferrand. Both queries look for a depot that does not exist: the list by its commune, the count by its name.

  • the list: no row, and the answer says the query may have missed the question
  • the count: 0, with the same doubt, never "0 customers"

Limits

  • The model picks the column. While the scenario was prepared, a depot named "Lyon" but located in Vénissieux was looked up by its commune: no row, and the answer said so, with no figure. Here, the depot is in Lyon.
  • For Clermont-Ferrand, the 7B's SQL varies with the scope. In another replay it measured from a raw connector table, which declares no point: both queries were refused before they ran. No figure either way.
  • "For each customer, which depot is nearest?" is not translated right by this model (qwen2.5-coder:7b): it joins customer and depot on the city.
  • The distance is as the crow flies, on a sphere: no route.
  • Synthetic customers and depots.

The customers of a legacy export, on a map

Goal Show the customers of a legacy export on a map on a phone, count those without a position, and never serve their coordinates.

A sales rep plans a round on a phone. The legacy ERP exports one GPS point per customer, with a decimal comma:

  • a list of 17 addresses does not say which are close;
  • a map does, and one tap opens the customer's record;
  • a customer with a broken point does not vanish from the map in silence.

What this step proves

The point is a declaration, not a guess on column names.

Limits

  • lm view does not show the verdict: the replay reads _map at the render door.
  • Without tiles, the grid draws a line every 10°: at the scale of a region, the scale bar is the only landmark.
  • With tiles, the tile server sees the areas viewed.
  • At most 1,000 rows on the map, and the map says so beyond.
  • No route, no distance on the map, no geocoding.
  • Synthetic customers.

The problem

A map can lie by omission.

  • A customer without a position vanishes.
  • Nobody sees it is missing.
  • The coordinates leave with the map.

The declaration

map:
  point: shop

One line in the view.

The point is the one the entity already declares.

The answers

16 customers placed, 1 counted apart.

  • The hub decides, row by row.
  • C-017 keeps its reason.
  • Latitude and longitude are not served.

The limit

Without tiles, few landmarks.

  • The default background is a grid.
  • A real map: the operator's tile server.
  • No route, no geocoding.

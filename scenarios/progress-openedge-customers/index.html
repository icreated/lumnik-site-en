Progress OpenEdge: the 1999 order that must not become 2099

Goal Read a Progress ERP's EXPORT files, two-digit years included, and be told when a dormant customer is reactivated.

A Progress OpenEdge ERP exports its Customer table with ABL's EXPORT: a .d file, 40 customers.

  • spaces, quoted strings, ? for unknown, yes/no, ISO-8859-1;
  • MM/DD/YY: a two-digit year must say its century, or validate refuses;
  • the reactivation campaign: two customers with no order since 1999 and 2003, two decisions.

The Customer table, as ABL exports it

FOR EACH Customer: EXPORT Customer. One customer per line.

  • one space between fields, strings in quotes, an inner quote doubled
  • ? for the unknown value
  • dates MM/DD/YY, logicals yes/no, ISO-8859-1
  1. 1
    -yy 1950

    Progress reads 99 as 1999 and 26 as 2026: its default century window starts in 1950.

MM/dd/yy without its century: refused

The first draft reads the dates with MM/dd/yy alone. lm validate refuses both dates before anything is read.

  • century-from is required with yy
  • the message names the parameter

    Before #1857

    The same draft was accepted, and 12/03/98 became 2098-12-03, without a cell error.

Reactivated, but dormant for a year

The Process listens on the export's table.

  • active=true: the customer is active again
  • last_order<2025-11-05: no order for a year
  • key: cust_num, the decision carries the customer number
  1. 1
    Why the century matters here

    Read as 2099, the 1999 order would never be "before 2025".

What this step proves

A rule on dates can be trusted once the dates can.

manifests/20-reactivated-dormant.yaml
apiVersion: apps.lumnik.io/v1kind: Processmetadata:  name: scenario-progress-dormant-aspec:  scope: sales-progress-a  on: { event: data.row.changed, table: connector.t_scenario_progress_a }  when:    - active=true    - last_order<2025-11-05          # no order for a year: check the credit before the first delivery  emit:    type: decision.reactivated-dormant-a    message: "{cust_num} {name} reactivated: last order {last_order}, customer since {since} — check its credit first"    key: cust_num

Limits

  • The rule's date is written in the manifest: "a year" is not computed from today.
  • Each day's export is kept: the table holds one row per customer per export.
  • An array field (EXTENT) is exported as several fields: each needs its own column name.
  • Synthetic customers, dates and balances.

Sales · Progress

The customers live in a Progress ERP.

Its export writes years on two digits.

03/14/99

The problem

98 used to become 2098.

  • Silently, without a cell error.
  • A 1999 order was never "over a year old".

What is not changed

The ERP stays as it is.

  • Its EXPORT, as ABL writes it.
  • No conversion, no 4GL code.

What had to be written

formats: ["MM/dd/yy"]
century-from: 1950

The window Progress itself uses.

  • Without it, validate refuses.
  • With it, 99 is 1999.

What was fixed

A two-digit year says its century (#1857).

  • Two reactivated customers: two decisions.
  • Before, they read 2099 and 2003.

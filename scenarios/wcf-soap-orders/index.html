SOAP WCF and WS-Security: the password rotated, the Fault says so

Goal Read a WCF service protected by a UsernameToken, survive the rotation of its password, and be told when an order is confirmed past its promised date.

A distributor exposes its open orders through a WCF service: HTTPS under the company's CA, a UsernameToken inside the envelope.

  • the CA and the password live in lm secret, never in the manifest;
  • the password rotates: over HTTP/2 the Fault is lost, over HTTP/1.1 it names the cause;
  • three orders confirmed past their promised date, three decisions.

A WCF order service, under the company's CA

BasicHttpBinding in TransportWithMessageCredential, UserName credential: the service requires a WS-Security UsernameToken inside the envelope.

  • HTTPS signed by the company's internal CA
  • GetOpenOrders(page, pageSize), the IsLastPage flag
  • the CA and the password go into lm secret
  1. 1
    CoreWCF

    The port of WCF to .NET 8. The binding and its defaults are WCF's; the service runs on Kestrel, not IIS.

30 open orders, three pages

The rest-generic connector, soap shape.

  • tls.ca_secret: this CA alone is trusted, for this connector
  • header: the UsernameToken, the password through {{secret:…}}
  • body: {{page}} and {{page_size}}, IsLastPage stops the loop
  1. 1
    No Timestamp

    CoreWCF accepts a UsernameToken without a wsu:Timestamp. It refuses an expired one: a timestamp frozen in the manifest would be refused the next day.

What this step proves

A WCF service secured inside the envelope is read without a proxy or .NET code.

Confirmed after the date promised to the customer

The Process listens to the connector's ext.* table.

  • confirmed_date>{promised_date}: two columns of the same row
  • key: order_no, the decision carries the order number
  1. 1
    Before the customer calls

    The decision arrives the morning the warehouse confirms, not the day the order goes missing.

What this step proves

A rule compares two dates of the same document, with no date written in.

The password rotated: I/O error, six requests

Next morning, IT rotates the account's password. The secret still holds the old one.

  • the service answers 500 and its Fault, then resets the HTTP/2 stream
  • the hub negotiates HTTP/2: the reset erases the answer already received
  • pre-flight and run: I/O error, each call retried
  1. 1

    Six POSTs over HTTP/2, six 500s, for one pre-flight and one run: the refused credential is retried like a network failure.

Over HTTP/1.1, the Fault names the cause

One line in the connector: http_version: "1.1".

  • the run fails with the service's faultstring
  • a Fault is not retried: two requests, one per door
  • the pre-flight still says only HTTP 500 (#1861)

    Quoted

    1.1 without quotes is a number to YAML: lm validate refuses it.

Limits

  • Replayed against CoreWCF on Kestrel. A .NET Framework WCF on IIS was not replayed: whether it accepts a UsernameToken without a Timestamp is not verified here.
  • The password travels in clear inside the envelope (PasswordText), protected by HTTPS. A PasswordDigest needs a nonce and a date on every call: no placeholder makes them.
  • The pre-flight reads HTTP 500 where the run names the Fault (#1861).
  • SOAP 1.1 only: a SOAP 1.2 service (wsHttpBinding) is not read.
  • Orders, customers and passwords synthetic.

Sales · WCF

The orders live behind a WCF service.

SOAP, HTTPS, a UsernameToken inside the envelope.

<wsse:UsernameToken>

The problem

The password rotated. The hub said: I/O error.

  • The service had answered its Fault, then reset the HTTP/2 stream.
  • Three attempts for one refused credential.

What is not changed

The service stays as it is.

  • Its binding, its CA, its contract.
  • No proxy, no .NET code.

What had to be written

http_version: "1.1"

One line in the connector.

  • The Fault arrives whole.
  • It is not retried.

What was fixed

The HTTP version is chosen per connector (#1859).

  • New secret, same manifest: the read resumes.
  • Three late orders: three decisions.

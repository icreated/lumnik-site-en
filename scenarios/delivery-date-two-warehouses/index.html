A delivery date each warehouse works out differently

Goal Promise the customer the date their warehouse can keep: one calculation rule, set per warehouse (country, lead time, closed days).

The promised date = order date plus N working days. But each warehouse counts its own way:

  • Lyon, in France: 3 days, two inventory days;
  • Liège, in Belgium: 5 days, other holidays.

The operator writes the rule once; the integrator sets it per warehouse.

The operator writes the rule once

A Kotlin script steps day by day from the order date.

  • it counts Monday to Friday only
  • it skips the country's holidays (country)
  • it skips the warehouse's closed days (closed)
  • it stops after lead_days working days
  1. 1
    Holidays

    The operator's 2026 calendar, in the script.

  2. 2
    Why a script

    No shipped rule counts working days.

What this step proves

The rule has one text and one fingerprint, however many warehouses there are.

manifests/promised-date.kts
// Operator-provided: the delivery date promised to a customer — the order date plus `lead_days` working days.// A working day is Monday to Friday, not a public holiday of the warehouse's `country`, and not one of the// warehouse's own `closed` days. The integrator sets all three in the hook's context.@Suppress("UNCHECKED_CAST")val cells = bindings["cells"] as MutableMap<String, Any?>val context = bindings["context"] as Map<*, *>// The operator's 2026 calendar: fixed days and the Easter-dependent ones (Easter Monday, Ascension, Whit Monday).val holidays = mapOf(    "FR" to setOf("2026-01-01", "2026-04-06", "2026-05-01", "2026-05-08", "2026-05-14", "2026-05-25",                  "2026-07-14", "2026-08-15", "2026-11-01", "2026-11-11", "2026-12-25"),    "BE" to setOf("2026-01-01", "2026-04-06", "2026-05-01", "2026-05-14", "2026-05-25",                  "2026-07-21", "2026-08-15", "2026-11-01", "2026-11-11", "2026-12-25"))val off = holidays.getValue(context["country"] as String) + (context["closed"] as List<*>? ?: emptyList<Any>()).map { it.toString() }var day = java.time.LocalDate.parse(cells["order_date"] as String)var left = (context["lead_days"] as Number).toInt()while (left > 0) {    day = day.plusDays(1)    if (day.dayOfWeek.value <= 5 && day.toString() !in off) left--}cells["promised_date"] = day.toString()cells["warehouse"] = context["warehouse"] as String

Lyon: France, 3 days, two inventory days

Lyon's connector names the script and passes it its settings.

  • kotlin: script: the same script for every warehouse
  • context: this warehouse's settings: country, lead time, closed days
  • lead_days: a number, closed: a list of dates

CMD-3101, ordered Thursday 30 April, skips 1 May, the weekend and the 5 May inventory: delivery on 7 May.

  1. 1
    7 May

    Without the inventory, it would be the 6th.

What this step proves

The lead time reaches the script as a number, the closed days as a list.

Each order, each warehouse's date

The entity puts both promises side by side.

  • match: order and warehouse, one row per pair
  • sources: Lyon and Liège, read from the same orders file

CMD-3102, ordered 12 May, crosses Ascension on 14 May in both countries: 18 May in Lyon, 20 May in Liège.

Limits

  • The holiday calendar is the one the operator writes in the script: they keep it up to date each year.
  • The lead time counts working days, not hours: an evening order leaves like a morning one.
  • Synthetic orders.

Customer service · delivery

When will the customer get it?

It depends on the warehouse.

Lyon: 3 days and inventories. Liège: 5 days and other holidays.

Synthetic orders

The problem

One rule, two calendars.

  • 8 May is a holiday in France, not in Belgium.
  • Lyon closes for inventory.
  • Each warehouse has its lead time.

Nothing is changed

The orders stay as they are.

  • The operator writes the rule once.
  • The integrator sets it per warehouse.
  • Each order gets its date.

What it took to write

context:
  country: FR
  lead_days: 3
  closed: ["2026-05-05"]

Lyon's settings, in four lines.

The whole scenario fits in 4 files, 83 lines, including a 23-line script.

The answers

One order, two right dates.

  • CMD-3101: 7 May in Lyon, 8 May in Liège.
  • CMD-3102: 18 May in Lyon, 20 May in Liège.

The limit

The calendar needs upkeep.

  • Holidays live in the operator's script.
  • Days, not hours.

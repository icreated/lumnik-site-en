Auditing how long orders stay in each status

The ERP keeps only today's status: In progress yesterday, Delivered today. Nothing on the wait in between. The ERP cannot be changed.

  • a Workflow follows each order by its key
  • each status change enters a journal, dated
  • an order that stays still too long alerts
  • the journal says how long each order stayed in each state.

Each order followed by its key

The Workflow watches the status column.

  • key: order_id: one observation per order
  • dormancy.after: 25m: alert after 25 minutes without a move
  • aliases: the known spellings of each status
  • EN ATT is not declared yet
  1. 1
    Tick

    The hub's sweep runs every 15 minutes. No runner triggers it.

  2. 2
    Why key

    Without a key, the hub sees a column. With order_id, it compares each order with its previous state.

Every move, and the time spent before it

lm observations transitions lists the moves, newest first.

  • ENTERED: when the order entered the state it left
  • OBSERVED: the tick that saw the move
  • IN_FROM_S: the seconds spent in the state left
  1. 1
    Lower bound

    For a first move, the time counts from the first observation.

  2. 2
    Filter

    --workflow, --doc: one order's history.

Limits

  • Moves seen at the sweep's pace: a duration is a multiple of the tick, and two moves between two ticks show as one.
  • For an order's first move, the time counts from its first observation: a lower bound.
  • The replay compresses nights into 15-minute ticks. Durations are minutes.
  • Synthetic data.

The problem

The status has no history.

  • Tonight's export replaces yesterday's.
  • The time spent in a state is lost.
  • The ERP cannot be changed.

Nothing is changed

lumnik compares each night with the previous one.

  • Each order is followed by its number.
  • Each status change enters a journal, dated.
  • Nothing is written to the ERP.

What had to be written

key: order_id
dormancy:
  after: 25m

One order followed, one alert if it stops moving.

The whole scenario fits in 3 declaration files, 73 lines.

The journal

7 moves, each with its waiting time.

  • C-01: entered, then in progress, then delivered.
  • C-02: cancelled.
  • Each line gives the time spent in the state left.

Real values, replay of 28 October 2026.

The orders that stand still

4 orders did not move. They alert.

  • C-06, C-08, C-09, C-11.
  • One alert per stall, not at every tick.

The business decides

EN ATT means in progress. It is not a move.

  • The business declares EN ATT as an alias.
  • C-08 and C-09 are renamed.
  • Their clock and their alert stay.

A sites export with broken GPS coordinates

Goal Read the GPS coordinates of a legacy export in decimal or in degrees-minutes-seconds, exchange a swapped pair, and empty every unusable point with its reason.

Dispatch takes over the delivery sites of a legacy ERP. Several tools typed the GPS points over the years:

  • a decimal comma here, degrees-minutes-seconds there;
  • a swapped pair, a 0;0 left by a geocoder;
  • metres where degrees belong.

The manifest makes one point per site, or says why there is none.

The operator writes how a coordinate is read

A Kotlin script reads the two cells together.

  • decimal comma or point, minus sign
  • hemisphere letter before or after, instead of a sign: S, W and O (Ouest, French for West) give a negative number
  • degrees-minutes-seconds, seconds optional: 48°50'12"N, 43°42'N, 43 17 42 N
  • a spreadsheet's typographic marks: 48°04′45″N
  • range: latitude between -90 and 90, longitude between -180 and 180
  • unreadable pair: both cells are emptied, coord_issue says why
  1. 1
    Why a script

    No shipped transformer reads a pair of coordinates.

  2. 2
    One pair, one count

    An emptied pair counts once, not twice.

Limits

  • Without within, a swapped pair with no letter is not seen, and 0;0 is written as a point. Not replayed here.
  • One rectangle: the Fort-de-France site is a real point, outside mainland France, and it is emptied.
  • Lambert 93 metres are not converted: the pair is emptied.
  • The remarks are the script's own words.
  • No distance, no route, no geocoding. The link was read in the page, not followed.
  • Synthetic sites.

Logistics · delivery sites

Where exactly is this site?

The export writes it many ways.

Comma, degrees-minutes-seconds, N, E, O letters, a swapped pair, 0;0.

Synthetic sites

The problem

A wrong point looks like a right one.

  • 5.3698;43.2965: Marseille, the wrong way round.
  • 0;0: a real point, off the coast of Africa.
  • 574357.82: metres, not degrees.

Nothing is modified

The export stays as it is.

  • A script reads each pair.
  • The manifest says where the sites are.
  • A doubtful point is emptied, with its reason.

What had to be written

context:
  within: [41.0, -5.5, 51.5, 10.0]
  swapped: fix

The rectangle the sites live in.

The whole scenario is 4 declaration files, 72 lines, and a 98-line script.

The answers

21 sites written, 13 usable points.

  • 48°50'12"N becomes 48.836667.
  • 3 swapped pairs put the right way round, and flagged.
  • 7 pairs emptied, each with its reason.

The limit

The rectangle is the integrator's.

  • Without it, a swapped pair passes.
  • Fort-de-France is outside the rectangle.
  • Metres are not converted.

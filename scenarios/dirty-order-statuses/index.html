Limits

  • An unchanged file is not read again, so not re-checked.
  • Aliases ignore case and surrounding spaces, not accents: “Livré” and “Livre” are two spellings.
  • A blank status is counted apart, not as a spelling.
  • lumnik does not fix the ERP; EN ATT is for the business to settle.
  • Synthetic data.

Orders · ERP

The same status is written 14 ways.

EC, encours, En cours, EN COURS: four ways to say “in progress”.

40 orders, exported every night.

Fictional orders · synthetic data

The problem

How many orders are in progress?

  • Counting “En cours” finds 5 orders.
  • There are 15, under four spellings.
  • The ERP cannot be changed.

Nothing is changed

The ERP keeps its statuses.

  • lumnik reads the nightly export.
  • It writes nothing to the ERP.
  • The meaning of each status is declared in lumnik.

What had to be written

- code: EN_COURS
  aliases: ["En cours", "encours", "EC"]

This line says EC means “En cours”.

The whole scenario fits in 3 declaration files, 68 lines.

What lumnik reports

The spellings nobody declared.

  • First declaration: 6 unknown spellings, on 15 orders.
  • After the business confirms: EN ATT remains, on 2 orders.

Real values, replay of 28 October 2026.

Who decides

The business decides. lumnik does not guess.

  • EN ATT: waiting for payment, or for stock?
  • Until the business answers, EN ATT stays listed.
  • A confirmed spelling becomes an alias, in one line.

The next night

Alert arrives on its own.

  • Night 2: a new spelling arrives, “En-cours”.
  • Import is enough: a message names it, on 2 orders.
  • Night 3: nothing new, no message.

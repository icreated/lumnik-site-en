Failed orders hidden in the logs

Goal Tie each error in the application log to its order, its customer and its current status, to know which customers to call back.

Customers call: their order "never went through".

  • the server writes a text log, one file a day;
  • the ERP knows the orders, their customer, their status;
  • nobody matches the two.

A business log, not a monitoring tool.

Yesterday's log, one line per row

The connector reads the text file as written.

  • lines: true: each whole line, no delimiter, no quotes
  • filter-where: keeps WARN and ERROR, reading the raw line
  • regex-extract: takes time, level, server, message, then the order (CMD-…) from the message
  • parse-date: reads the milliseconds, Paris time
  • truncate: cuts an overlong message to 120 characters
  1. 1
    47 dropped

    INFO, stack traces, a quote never closed: counted in SKIPPED.

  2. 2
    Why lines: true

    A log is not a CSV: an orphan quote would break the read.

The orders hit, with customer and status

An entity starts from the log lines and joins the order they name.

  • match: timestamp and server, one log line = one incident
  • joins: order_no ties the incident to the order, which brings customer and status

Four timeouts:

  • CMD-1007 and CMD-1013: still pending
  • CMD-1022: went through on retry
  • CMD-1031: cancelled

Limits

  • A business log, not a monitoring tool: one file a day, reread whole; no tailing, no retention.
  • A stack trace is not attached to its error: its lines are dropped and counted.
  • Synthetic log and orders.

One customer typed twice found by its GPS point in the legacy export

Goal Find the customer accounts opened twice for the same shop, which the ERP cannot see because the address is typed another way, with the answer's SQL.

The legacy ERP compares typed addresses: "12 rue Garibaldi" and "12, r. Garibaldi – Bât B" make two customers, two credit lines, two dunning runs. Each account also carries a GPS point.

  • by the address: no pair
  • by the place: 3 pairs within 50 m
  • a person decides: the same place is not always the same customer

The customer accounts export, coordinates read at the door

One CSV file, one connector. The coordinates arrive with a decimal comma.

  • parse-coordinates: reads latitude and longitude together, as numbers
  • within: south, west, north, east. The rectangle of metropolitan France
  • reason: the column that says why a point was emptied
  1. 1
    17 = 17

    Every account is written, with or without a point.

  2. 2
    C-016

    0;0, outside the rectangle: emptied and counted.

What this step proves

Coordinates become numbers at the door; an unusable point is emptied with its reason.

3 pairs of accounts within 50 m of each other

The answer shows its SQL: the account is joined to itself, and connector.distance_m measures between the two points.

  • C-001 and C-011, 6 m apart: the same bakery
  • C-002 and C-015, 18 m apart: the same garage
  • C-003 and C-004, 26 m apart: a pharmacy and an optician in the same mall

Limits

  • C-016 is the joinery of C-007, typed a second time with 0;0. With no point, it is compared with nothing: the place only finds accounts that have one.
  • The wording matters. While preparing the scenario on an isolated hub, "Which customers are less than 50 m from another customer?" got an abstention from the model (qwen2.5-coder:7b), with no SQL. "For each customer, which other customers are less than 50 m from it?" is translated right. Without "de lui" ("from it") it is refused too: the question hangs on two words. Both were asked in French.
  • Each pair comes back twice, once from each side: 6 rows for 3 pairs.
  • 50 m is a choice. In a dense area two different customers are closer; on a large site the same customer can be further apart.
  • Synthetic accounts.

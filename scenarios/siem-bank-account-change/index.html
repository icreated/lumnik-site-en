A supplier bank-account change, silent in the ERP, reaches the SIEM.

The ERP exports its suppliers as CSV and emits no event. lumnik rereads the export on a schedule, emits a signed decision by webhook, and the SIEM correlates: one account change, one high alert. The inbox never shows the account.

The account goes to the SIEM, not into the sentence

A kind:Process observes every active supplier. Its message names the supplier; fields: [iban] has the signed payload carry the account, for the SIEM alone.

    Signed

A baseline, then an alert

The SIEM takes each supplier's first account as its baseline, then alerts on a different fingerprint: SUP-0042, …0189 → …0458, high severity.

  1. 1
    Fingerprint

    The SIEM keeps only the account's SHA-256 and its last four digits.

  2. 2
    Idempotent

    A replayed event id does not alert twice.

What this step proves

Every step leaves a trace to read.

Limits

  • The Process sees a new ingested row; it does not compare it with the previous one. The SIEM does the temporal correlation, as in production.
  • The full account crosses the signed webhook boundary so the SIEM can compute an exact fingerprint: in production, HTTPS, and restricted access to the delivery journal, which keeps payloads.
  • The SIEM is a synthetic stand-in; a real SIEM would apply its own rules.
  • Synthetic data.
